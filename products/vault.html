The problem

Knowledge that lives in pipelines evaporates.

Parsed documents, extracted facts and hard-won context end up scattered across buckets, vector stores and notebooks — unversioned, ungoverned and invisible to the next system that needs them. Storage keeps files. It does not keep knowledge.

scattered outputsno permissionsno historyno audit traillocked to one stack
Two jobs, one pipeline

Knowledge produces. Vault preserves.

Parse and Knowledge can return results through an API and stop there. Vault is what turns those results into a living system: the accumulated, versioned state of everything your organization knows — ready to evolve when the next source arrives.

Orves Knowledge — interprets

  • reads structured evidence
  • identifies claims and entities
  • produces relationships and provenance
  • adjudicates against the evidence

Its job ends when knowledge is produced.

Orves Vault — preserves

  • keeps the accumulated, versioned state
  • compares new knowledge with existing context
  • records every change and why it happened
  • serves the current state — with its history

Its job never ends: knowledge stays alive, governed and served.

The incremental loop

Every new source becomes part of the existing context.

A new source is parsed, turned into knowledge, and compared against what the Vault already holds. Nothing is blindly appended — and nothing is silently overwritten.

new source→Parse→Knowledge→compare with existing knowledge→add · confirm · refine · update · contradict→new version in Vault

Every outcome — including contradiction — is written as a new version. Prior states keep their evidence.

Disagreement, preserved

Contradictions are knowledge, not errors.

Two institutions forecast the same indicator and disagree. Vault does not average them, overwrite one, or compress competing views into an artificial consensus — each claim keeps its author, date and evidence, visible and auditable.

The relationship vocabulary

How new knowledge relates to what you already know.

A later source may update an earlier position, contradict another institution, refine a prior claim or introduce an unrelated view on the same topic. Vault preserves those distinctions instead of silently merging them.

Evidence-backed knowledge graph

Every relationship keeps its evidence.

Entities and claims are not connected because they look semantically similar. Every relationship retains provenance, source references, temporal context and the evidence that produced it.
