The problem

Knowledge that lives in pipelines evaporates.

Parsed documents, extracted facts and hard-won context end up scattered across buckets, vector stores and notebooks — unversioned, ungoverned and invisible to the next system that needs them. Storage keeps files. It does not keep knowledge.

Why Vault exists

Prove produces. Vault preserves.

Parse structures your sources. Prove reads incoming evidence, compares it with the current Vault state and produces traceable claims, relationships and proposed changes. Vault commits, versions and preserves the resulting knowledge state — together with its evidence, history and disagreements.

Orves Prove — the transformation

  • reads incoming evidence
  • compares it with the current Vault state
  • produces claims, entities and relationships
  • grounds every proposed claim and relationship in evidence

Each Prove run produces a traceable result.

Orves Vault — the state

  • commits proposed changes as new versions
  • preserves evidence, history and disagreements
  • governs who can see and change what
  • serves the current state — with its history

Vault preserves those results across runs and over time.

The incremental loop
new source→Parse→Prove reads the current Vault state→compares incoming evidence→proposes add · confirm · refine · update · contradict→Vault commits a new version

Current state, evidence and history remain available — nothing is blindly appended, nothing is silently overwritten.

Disagreement, preserved

Contradictions are knowledge, not errors.

Two institutions forecast the same indicator and disagree. Prove relates the two claims; Vault preserves both — with author, date and evidence — instead of averaging them, overwriting one, or manufacturing consensus.

The relationship vocabulary

A later source may update an earlier position, contradict another institution, refine a prior claim or introduce an unrelated view on the same topic. Prove proposes the relationship; Vault preserves the distinction — never a silent merge.

Inside a vault

More than files. More than vectors. More than a graph.

A vault holds the sources, the knowledge produced from them, and the infrastructure that keeps both governed. The knowledge graph is a capability inside the Vault — not the product.

What makes knowledge usable and governable at scale.

Evidence-backed knowledge graph

Entities and claims are not connected because they look semantically similar. Every relationship retains provenance, source references, temporal context and the evidence that produced it.
